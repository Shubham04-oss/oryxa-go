# LinkedIn

## Why here

The team buyer lives here: heads of operations, chiefs of staff, engineering
managers, heads of customer success, founders of 20–150 person companies.
They read posts, they accept connection notes that name their problem, and
they reply to a DM that isn't a pitch. It is the main outbound channel for
design partners, and in India it is the business network.

## Who is there

The champion from [design-partners.md](../design-partners.md#who-qualifies),
and the people who introduce us to them. Also the Indian startup ecosystem,
investors (not a target; they watch), and the press.

## The thing

**Founder-led posts, three a week, and ten DMs a day from the list.** A post
is 150–250 words, text first, one image or clip, three hashtags at most, the
first line the hook. Monday a story, Wednesday an insight, Friday a demo or
a number. Comments are sentences on ten ICP posts a day, never "great post".
A connection note names their problem and has no link.

**The three kinds of post.**
- **Monday, a story:** a call (anonymised, with the partner's words), a
  failure (the deploy that stopped, the ask the screen refused), why the
  founder is building this (the Slack black hole, told as a day).
- **Wednesday, an insight:** AI is single-player and work isn't; consent beats indexing; the two airbags; why an
  agent should be you and not a service account; what "unverified app"
  means and why we're in Google's review; what ten teams taught us.
- **Friday, a demo or a number:** the clip; this week's asks across partners;
  time to approve; the release.

## Rules

- No hashtag soup. No reposting an X post verbatim: a buyer needs the
  problem first.
- A connection note never carries a link. A DM carries one, after the second
  sentence.
- No DM to anyone who hasn't shown the problem (a post, a question, a job ad
  that mentions cross-team work, a #help channel visible in a stack page).
- No pitching in someone else's comments. Answer the question; the profile
  does the rest.
- The company page posts releases and the ship log, nothing else. The
  founder's profile is the channel.
- Partners are named only with written permission, and never their asks.

## The first ten moves

1. The founder's headline: the bio line from [positioning.md](../positioning.md#one-liners-by-length).
   The About: the story in order, 120 words. Featured: the demo, the partner
   page, the repo.
2. The company page: logo, the bio line, the website, "Software development".
3. The ICP list: 30 India, 30 US/EU, with the one-line reason each, in the
   private pipeline.
4. Post 1, Monday: why I'm building this (the day the answer was behind a
   permission wall).
5. Post 2, Wednesday: AI is single-player and work isn't.
6. Post 3, Friday: the sixty-second clip.
7. Week 3: fifty connection notes from the list, ten a day.
8. Week 4: the carousel.
9. Week 5: the partner post ("ten teams") with the numbers so far.
10. Week 6: the first anonymised partner story, with their permission.

## What we count

Weekly: impressions per post, comments by ICP titles, connection acceptance
rate, DM reply rate, calls booked, installs that started here. The last two
are the ones that matter.

## Founder or agent

Agents research, draft, build the carousel, keep the list. The founder
comments, posts, DMs, and takes every call.
